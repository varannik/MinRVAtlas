# Two Registries Gate Kiln-to-Rock Credits Differently

Puro.earth and Isometric take a cement-kiln BioCCS facility with in-situ mineralisation through the same sequence: qualify, pass KYC and sign a master agreement, register, submit a package for the registry's pre-review, host a registry-appointed and registry-paid VVB for a combined validation and first verification with a site visit, then receive credits ex-post once the registry accepts the VVB's report. **Neither will validate a project before CO2 is being injected.** **Puro charges the supplier** (€1,400 a year plus a per-CORC service fee, or a flat €12,000 when an audit yields 1,000 CORCs or fewer), takes its evidence as an 11-module folder of Word and Excel templates under GSC Edition 2024 v5, and explicitly accepts both biogenic CO2 from kilns that co-fire coal and biomass and dissolved-CO2 injection into ultramafic rock. **Isometric charges the buyer** ($10/t on the closed-system schedule that BioCCS falls under), takes its evidence as answers to a requirement list generated in its Certify platform, and plans on about three months from first PDD draft to first issuance. Each registry has one hard gate for this facility. Puro accepts storage outside the US, EEA, UK and three Canadian provinces only after it approves the host country's legal framework against an eight-point table, at its own discretion and before the audit. Isometric's BioCCS protocol never names cement, asks for a justification wherever fossil fuel exceeds 5% of feedstock mass, and needs a licensed geoscientist to sign off any storage permit issued outside five named regimes. For the existing UAE package, **the binding gate is jurisdiction on Puro and source eligibility on Isometric**, and each should be settled in writing before audit money is spent. Both registries ban double registration, and Puro refuses any project that failed another scheme's audit in the past three years, so the two routes have to be tried one after the other, not run in parallel.

## Both paths run about nine steps, with validation and first verification done together

The rulebooks changed during 2026, and the versions matter because each registry locks the version in force when the supplier commits. Puro's binding gates now sit in the program-agnostic **Certification Procedures v1.2 (30 Sep 2026)**, the Puro Program Annex v1.0 and the Common Criteria v1.0. These run alongside **General Rules v4.4 (7 May 2026)** and the **GSC methodology Edition 2024 v5 (approved 23 Apr 2026)** ([CP v1.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Earth%20Certification%20Procedures%20v1.2%20FINAL.pdf); [Puro Document Library](https://puro.earth/cdr-infrastructure/methodologies/document-library/); [GSC 2024 v5](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Geologically_Stored_Carbon_2024_5.pdf)). On the Isometric side the governing set is **Standard v2.2, the BioCCS Protocol v1.5 and the In-situ Mineralization Module v1.2**, which was updated in the 12 May 2026 Spring release ([Isometric Standard](https://registry.isometric.com/standard); [BioCCS v1.5](https://registry.isometric.com/protocol/biogenic-capture-and-storage/1.5); [Spring 2026 update](https://isometric.com/writing-articles/spring-2026-update)). The Isometric protocol is BioCCS, not Direct Air Capture: BioCCS v1.5 lists in-situ mineralisation v1.2 among its accepted storage modules ([BioCCS v1.5](https://registry.isometric.com/protocol/biogenic-capture-and-storage)).

For Isometric it draws on the [onboarding FAQ](https://docs.isometric.com/support/working-with-isometric/onboarding-and-timelines.md), the [Certify validation guide](https://docs.isometric.com/user-guides/certify/validation.md), the [project design guide](https://docs.isometric.com/user-guides/certify/project-design.md) and the [site-visit FAQ](https://docs.isometric.com/support/validation-and-verification/site-visits.md).

The two registries place their gates differently. Puro's gates are legal and checked for completeness. KYC has to clear before the Platform Agreement can be signed (CP 4.2.1–4.2.2). Puro then reviews whether the package is "complete and sufficient to attempt a Validation and/or Verification audit" before it allocates a VVB, and it can deregister a project that is still insufficient "after multiple revision cycles" (CP 5.2.1–5.2.4) ([CP v1.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Earth%20Certification%20Procedures%20v1.2%20FINAL.pdf)). Isometric's gates are built into its software and followed by a public test. Certify's progress bar blocks submission until every requirement is answered and every Remi issue is closed. Isometric's scientists then approve the submission iteratively. Afterwards the registry itself publishes the PDD for comment. On Puro, by contrast, the supplier runs its own stakeholder consultation of at least 14 days after registration approval and before the audit ([Isometric project design](https://docs.isometric.com/user-guides/certify/project-design.md); [Isometric Standard](https://registry.isometric.com/standard); [Puro SEP Requirements](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Stakeholder%20Engagement%20Requirements.pdf)). Isometric also requires a supplier-led consultation under Standard §3.8, so an Isometric project carries both burdens.

Neither registry offers stand-alone validation before operation for this facility. GSC 2.2.1 requires the capture and storage sites to be **operational at the Facility Audit** ([GSC 2024 v5](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Geologically_Stored_Carbon_2024_5.pdf)). Isometric's VVB must observe business-as-usual operations, and the site visit "usually takes place after you submit your GHG statement" ([Isometric site visits](https://docs.isometric.com/support/validation-and-verification/site-visits.md)). The practical plan on either registry is therefore to start injecting, run a short first monitoring period, and submit facility and output evidence together. CP 5.4.4 allows the first verification to run "in tandem with the Validation audit upon request by the Operator" ([CP v1.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Earth%20Certification%20Procedures%20v1.2%20FINAL.pdf)). The only formal outputs available before operation are Puro's Preliminary Assessment, which gives eligibility confirmation, early identification of gaps and a facility listing that investors can see ([Puro Preliminary Assessment](https://puro.earth/preliminary-assessment)), and Isometric's eligibility report.

Failure carries a higher cost on Puro. Under CP section 6, a **critical or major non-conformity at validation means the project is Rejected, and it can re-apply only after 12 months**. Major findings at verification suspend the Certificate of Compliance and must be remediated within 90 days or the project is withdrawn. GR 4.4 still describes the older regime of "30 days, one resubmission", so the two rulebooks overlap ([CP v1.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Earth%20Certification%20Procedures%20v1.2%20FINAL.pdf); [GR v4.4](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/General%20Rules%204_4.pdf)). Isometric logs findings as ISO 14064-3 issue types that must be closed before validation, except Forward Action Requests. Where a requirement does not fit the project, Isometric can approve an Explanatory Note "at Isometric's discretion" ([Isometric log of issues](https://docs.isometric.com/user-guides/certify/log-of-issues.md); [Explanatory Notes FAQ](https://docs.isometric.com/support/validation-and-verification/audit-exemptions-and-explanatory-notes.md)). For a kiln source that the BioCCS protocol does not name, that discretionary route may be how eligibility gets settled.

## Puro bills the supplier, Isometric bills the buyer, and both hire the auditor

**Agreements.** Each registry has a single master contract, and neither publishes its text. On Puro, signing the Platform Agreement makes the supplier an Account Holder. It commits the supplier to certification and to the fee schedule, and fixes the methodology version: "the applicable version of each methodology … shall be determined and fixed at the time of signature of the Platform Agreement" (CP 4.2.3). The supplier must report every sale to the registry and pay the service fee wherever it trades. On termination it must still complete "any Audits still due" and pay outstanding fees. The registry account opens automatically once the agreement is signed and fees are paid ([CP v1.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Earth%20Certification%20Procedures%20v1.2%20FINAL.pdf); [GR v4.4](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/General%20Rules%204_4.pdf)). Isometric's equivalent is the **Supplier Agreement plus a project appendix**, after which "your project is committed to issuing with Isometric". A partner company can be named in the PDD without signing ([Isometric onboarding FAQ](https://docs.isometric.com/support/working-with-isometric/onboarding-and-timelines.md)). Because neither text is public, the governing law, liability for reversals, exclusivity and data-licence terms remain unknown until the supplier asks for a draft.

**KYC.** Puro's process is the heavier of the two. Its KYB/KYC/AML Policies v1.2 (28 Apr 2026) collect company identity and an authorised representative. They verify the company through its registration certificate and register of shareholders, and may also ask for annual reports, financial statements, an EIA or third-party certifications. Puro screens against EU and UN sanctions lists and World-Check, and keeps monitoring after approval. A simplified tier exists only for entities "subject to statutory reporting, continuous disclosure, and regulatory oversight". The authorised representative must also prove their signatory rights ([Puro KYB/KYC/AML v1.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Puro%20Earth%20KYC-AML%20Policies%20V1.2.pdf)). An unlisted special-purpose vehicle or joint venture should expect full KYB. One person should serve as the KYC'd representative, the Platform Agreement signatory and the GR 2.2.4.3 signatory who attests to the package's accuracy. GSC also requires a certified trade registry extract for the supplier and for **every external operator**, which here means the capture operator, the haulier and the storage operator (GSC 3.3.1, 3.3.5) ([GSC 2024 v5](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Geologically_Stored_Carbon_2024_5.pdf)). Isometric's KYC Policy v1.1 (8 Jul 2024) asks for four items: legal name, registered address, company number and industry sector. It screens these against FATF high-risk jurisdictions, watchlists and UN sanctions, and checks beneficial owners only when activity looks suspicious ([Isometric KYC Policy v1.1](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1YLopoLcxZojb1-6rO8d6bzLwW05cIkdX/view)).

**Auditor.** Neither registry lets the supplier pick or pay the VVB. Puro "shall allocate a VVB following a successful review of the submitted documentation … and the Operator shall confirm acceptance" (CP 5.2.2). Puro pays for verification, VVB reports reach Puro before they reach the operator, and Puro observes about 2% of audits ([CP v1.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Earth%20Certification%20Procedures%20v1.2%20FINAL.pdf); [Puro certification](https://puro.earth/cdr-infrastructure/carbon-credit-certification/)). Isometric "contracts and pays the VVB on your behalf" after a public RFP. Bids are judged on knowledge of the pathway, familiarity with the region, availability, and price. A supplier may state a preference but "cannot contract the VVB directly" ([Isometric VVB selection](https://docs.isometric.com/support/validation-and-verification/vvb-selection.md)). Isometric also enforces rotation: one VVB for **no more than five consecutive years, and in no more than five of any seven** ([Isometric VVB Policy v1.2](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1KOmLuhU6yzCYYtUWPyQCw4aByEEJ7Q1-/view)). On both registries, a VVB that advised on the project cannot audit it, so any engineering or LCA consultant the supplier uses is barred from auditing.

On Puro the supplier's first cash outlay depends on how long the first monitoring period runs. A short first period that yields 1,000 CORCs or fewer triggers the €12,000 flat fee at audit booking, before any credit exists. A longer period moves the supplier onto per-CORC fees, which are not due until the credits are sold. Unpaid invoices are grounds for suspension (CP Table 4, 6.1.6). On Isometric the fee follows the protocol rather than the storage module, so BioCCS should sit on the $10/t closed-system schedule and not on the "Carbon Mineralization" open-system schedule, which starts at **$30/t**. That inference should be confirmed with Isometric, because the calculator lists both pathways ([Isometric fee calculator](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/65ef0b18cfe3fb84ec002a81/6af2132e3d8a4d27b0d81555_Isometric%20verification%20fee%20calculator.xlsx)). Isometric's 1% buffer works as a quiet cost to the supplier. A UAE storage site is unlikely to qualify for the "equivalent regulatory scheme" exemption unless Isometric accepts the local regime as equivalent.

Both registries allow delegation, with limits. Consultants and dMRV partners can draft the package. Puro's dMRV Connect API (beta) supports Preliminary Assessment, Facility Audit and Output Audit frameworks when the supplier grants access in MyPuro ([dMRV Connect](https://docs.api.puro.earth/dmrv/overview); [applying frameworks](https://docs.api.puro.earth/dmrv/guides/setting-up-monitoring-periods/applying-frameworks)). Whether a GSC framework is exposed through it is unconfirmed, so plan for submission through the MyPuro interface. Isometric gives consultants a "Custom access" role, and its Preview accounts already have full access to the MRV API ([Isometric account roles](https://docs.isometric.com/user-guides/account-roles.md); [Certify and data FAQ](https://docs.isometric.com/support/platform/certify-and-data.md)). The supplier must still act itself on several points: KYC, signing the agreement, holding exclusive rights to the credits, signing the accuracy attestation, accepting the VVB (on Puro), and notifying reversals.

## Commitment-date and 12-month reporting clocks set the real deadlines

| Clock | Puro.earth | Isometric |
|---|---|---|
| Version lock | Fixed at Platform Agreement signature. The operator can elect the latest version before validation starts | Latest certified protocol at application, locked for the crediting period. Later versions are optional until renewal |
| Validation deadline | **3 years from the Commitment Date** (GR 2.2.3; AAR 2.1) **or 5 years for geological-storage projects such as BioCCS** (CP 5.3.9). The two conflict | No deadline stated. The crediting-period start date can be no earlier than **5 years before the first registry submission** |
| Crediting period | **15 years** from the first day of the first monitoring period, renewable twice | **15 years** for BioCCS, renewable without limit after re-validation |
| First reporting period | Up to 18 months (GR 2.4.2) or 24 months (CP 5.4.5). The two conflict | Any period from the validated start date |
| Ongoing cadence | An output report at least every 12 months. The number of reports per year is capped by volume: 1 below 10,000 CORCs, rising to 12 at 60,000 or more | Verification at least annually. Monthly issuance is marketed |
| Lateness | Over 12 months late: suspended. Over 18 months: withdrawn, and a new validation is needed | More than 12 months without verification triggers a PDD update and re-validation |
| Time to first credit | No official figure. One GSC biogas project was announced on 21 Jul 2026 with more than 700 CORCs for Feb–May 2026 | About 3 months from the first PDD draft for an operating project; final V&V report 6–8 weeks after the site visit |
| Reversal notice | Within 5 days | Within 1 business day (3 at most) |

Sources: [CP v1.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Earth%20Certification%20Procedures%20v1.2%20FINAL.pdf), [GR v4.4](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/General%20Rules%204_4.pdf), [GSC 2024 v5](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Geologically_Stored_Carbon_2024_5.pdf), [AAR v2.1](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Standard%20Additionality%20Assessment%20Requirements%20v2.1.pdf), [Puro fees](https://puro.earth/certified-carbon-credits/fees/), [Mondo Visione, 21 Jul 2026](https://mondovisione.com/news/puroearth-certifies-worlds-first-permanent-carbon-removal-from-biogas-producti-2026721/), [Isometric Standard](https://registry.isometric.com/standard), [Isometric v2.0 announcement](https://isometric.com/writing-articles/isometric-certifies-version-2-0-of-the-standard) and the [Isometric onboarding FAQ](https://docs.isometric.com/support/working-with-isometric/onboarding-and-timelines.md).

The most dangerous of these clocks is one that may already be running. Puro defines the **Commitment Date** as the date the supplier "committed to implementing the CO2 Removal activity (e.g., the date when contracts for the purchase or installation of equipment … were signed)" ([GR v4.4](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/General%20Rules%204_4.pdf)). GR 4.4 and the Baseline and Additionality Questionnaire v2.0 both require design validation within 3 years of that date. CP v1.2 allows 5 years for projects that need "development and permitting of geological CO2 storage infrastructure, specifically such as BioCCS and DACCS". The Common Criteria add a requirement to submit for validation no later than two years after the activity start date, unless the VVB accepts a justification ([CP v1.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Earth%20Certification%20Procedures%20v1.2%20FINAL.pdf); [Common Criteria v1.0](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Earth%20Common%20Criteria%20v1.0.pdf); [Baseline and Additionality Questionnaire v2.0](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Puro%20Baseline%20and%20Additionality%20Questionnaire%20-%20v2.0.docx)). No published Puro document ranks GR 4.4 against CP v1.2 where they conflict. The local package already documents capture-equipment installation (mechanical completion and torquing reports) but contains no evidence of the Commitment Date ([local audit package](../docs/1.%20Final%20Submission/)). The supplier should therefore fix that date from the equipment contract and ask Puro for a written ruling on which window applies. CP 4.3.4 gives such written confirmations the same standing as a published compatibility matrix.

The clocks also set how much past injection can earn credits. On Puro the crediting period starts on the first day of the first monitoring period, and that first period can cover at most 18 or 24 months. Earlier injections cannot be credited. Isometric lets GHG statements reach back to a start date up to five years before the first submission, provided the data meets protocol requirements ([Isometric Standard](https://registry.isometric.com/standard)). For a facility that is already injecting, Isometric's rules therefore allow much more historical credit. The catch is that Isometric "typically only" takes on late-entry projects "with offtakes in place, or that meet minimum tonnage thresholds" ([Isometric onboarding FAQ](https://docs.isometric.com/support/working-with-isometric/onboarding-and-timelines.md)). In practice, a signed buyer is the entry ticket for an operating facility.

Issuance frequency follows volume. A Puro facility below 10,000 CORCs a year gets **one output report a year**. More frequent audits require Issuance Plus, which needs at least 1,000 CORCs per audit and supports quarterly, monthly or even weekly cycles. Under Clarification #028, the auditor may decide that frequent output audits need no site visit ([Puro fees](https://puro.earth/certified-carbon-credits/fees/); [Puro Clarifications](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Clarifications%20Puro%20Standard%2006%20Nov%202026.pdf)). Puro issues whole tonnes and carries any fraction forward to the next issuance. Isometric issues each GHG entry as its own batch in kilogram units, and takes the vintage from the year in which the entry ends ([CP v1.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Earth%20Certification%20Procedures%20v1.2%20FINAL.pdf); [Isometric GHG entries](https://docs.isometric.com/user-guides/certify/ghg-entry.md); [Isometric registry concepts](https://docs.isometric.com/user-guides/registry/key-registry-concepts.md)). Isometric's three-month planning figure is more credible than its marketing claim of credits "within a month" ([isometric.com/suppliers](https://isometric.com/suppliers)). A first-of-kind kiln source with a non-listed storage permit should plan for longer.

## Puro wants an eleven-module package; Isometric wants a generated checklist answered

The two registries ask for the same evidence in different forms. **Puro's package is a fixed set of folders built from templates.** Under GR 2.2.4.2 the project description must cover 11 items: name, location, methodology and supplier; a non-technical description; a technical description; quantification with uncertainty; reversal risk; conformity to the methodology; the monitoring plan; the Baseline and Additionality Assessment; stakeholder consultation; E&S safeguards; and SDG impacts. It must be signed by "an individual with legal signing authority" ([GR v4.4](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/General%20Rules%204_4.pdf)). Project Description template v2.1 turns these items into tables with **hard word limits**: at most 200 words of non-technical description, 500 words of technical description, 250 each for scope, baseline and additionality, 300 for quantification, 500 each for the monitoring plan, LCA and E&S, and 150 for SDGs. It also asks for WGS84 coordinates for every key location, business IDs for every operator, and up to three illustrations with publication rights. The description is submitted as both PDF and Word and is published ([Puro Project Description template](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Project%20Description.docx)).

The other current templates are the **Baseline and Additionality Questionnaire v2.0** (Jul 2026), which replaces the separate additionality questionnaire; the **ESS Questionnaire v2.2**, answered Yes/No against GR 6.4 rule numbers with evidence behind each answer; the Stakeholder Engagement Report template; the SDG reporting template v1.2; and an LCA model workbook that follows the LCA Guidelines for Suppliers ([Baseline and Additionality Questionnaire v2.0](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Puro%20Baseline%20and%20Additionality%20Questionnaire%20-%20v2.0.docx); [ESS Questionnaire v2.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro_Env_&_Soc_Assess.docx); [SDG template](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20SDG%20reporting%20template.docx); [LCA Guidelines](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro_LCA-Guidelines-for-Suppliers.pdf)). The package's Audit Document Index groups the evidence into 11 modules: Administrative, Additionality and baseline, E&S safeguards, Capture site, Transport and logistics, Storage site, Monitoring plan, Leakage, LCA, SDGs and Project description. A "Contact information to auditor" workbook sits at the root ([local audit package](../docs/1.%20Final%20Submission/)).

**Isometric has no static template.** The PDD is a list of requirements that Certify generates from the Standard, the protocol and each selected module. Every requirement carries a shortcode such as `R-NK7R-0`, and the list is grouped into five sections: Project setup, Protocol & monitoring, Environmental & social, Stakeholder input, and Pathway specific. Each piece of evidence needs a category (one of 11), a stated reason and page references ([Isometric project design](https://docs.isometric.com/user-guides/certify/project-design.md)). Isometric's guidance warns that VVBs "typically raise an issue against any sub-requirement that is missing or unanswered", and that attachments "should not replace a written explanation" ([PDD quality FAQ](https://docs.isometric.com/support/validation-and-verification/pdd-quality-best-practice.md)). The LCA is built in Certify from the protocol's default template, using components such as a "Transport emissions" blueprint (mass × distance × factor). It is then converted into a GHG-entry template. Each GHG entry must be net-negative on its own, and the first GHG statement needs a sensitivity analysis and a written report ([Isometric LCA](https://docs.isometric.com/user-guides/certify/lca.md); [GHG statements](https://docs.isometric.com/user-guides/certify/ghg-statement.md)). The exact requirement list for the BioCCS plus in-situ combination appears only once the project exists in Certify. A free Preview account can generate and export it today ([Certify and data FAQ](https://docs.isometric.com/support/platform/certify-and-data.md)).

## Kiln radiocarbon, truck tickets and bubble points decide both audits

### The biogenic fraction must be measured on the captured stream

A cement kiln emits CO2 from three sources: biomass fuel, fossil fuel and limestone calcination. Only the first can earn credits. Puro has settled whether this source qualifies at all: Clarification #022 (30 Oct 2025) says rule 3.2.3(b) "shall not be interpreted as making non-eligible for CORC issuance the use of biogenic carbon resulting from the co-fire of coal and eligible biomass in cement clinker production" ([Puro Clarifications](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Clarifications%20Puro%20Standard%2006%20Nov%202026.pdf)). Because the stream is mixed, GSC 4.4.5 requires F_eligible source to be measured by **radiocarbon analysis under ISO 13833 or ASTM D6866, at least semi-annually**. That fraction is then multiplied by the eligible-biomass fraction from delivery records, and CORCs may never exceed the eligible CO2 captured in the same period ([GSC 2024 v5](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Geologically_Stored_Carbon_2024_5.pdf)).

Isometric is stricter on both points. BioCCS v1.5 lists biomass power, combined heat and power, bio-hydrogen, biogas and energy-from-waste as example sources and never mentions cement. Projects where fossil fuels exceed 5% of feedstock mass "must provide a justification" (`R-YJJC-0`). Where a waste feedstock mixes biogenic and fossil carbon inseparably, as refuse-derived fuel (RDF) does, the project must use **Method A**: continuous flue-gas sampling proportional to stack mass flow, integrated over no more than three months, with radiocarbon analysis. An estimated average F_B must be in the PDD at validation (`R-0T9J-0`) ([BioCCS v1.5](https://registry.isometric.com/protocol/biogenic-capture-and-storage/1.5)). The mass-balance allocation route requires the fossil emissions to sit under an approved cap-and-trade scheme, and only the EU ETS and UK ETS are approved. A UAE kiln therefore cannot use that route ([BioCCS v1.5](https://registry.isometric.com/protocol/biogenic-capture-and-storage)). Cement eligibility should be the first question for Isometric's optional eligibility assessment.

### Biomass evidence runs per delivery, and tyres have no home

Puro's Biomass Sourcing Criteria v1.3 sort RDF into category A, which requires an ID, date, amount, waste type, area and delivering entity for every delivery. End-of-life wood falls into category C, which also needs the material grade and, for hazardous wood, an authorisation to process it ([Biomass Sourcing Criteria v1.3](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Clarification/Puro%20Biomass%20Sourcing%20Criteria%20v1.3.pdf)). The criteria have **no category for end-of-life tyres**. The biogenic share of tyre-derived fuel needs Puro's acceptance before the audit, or it should be excluded through F_eligible biomass.

Isometric's Biomass Feedstock Accounting v1.4 asks for more in the same area. Every feedstock supplier needs registration details and a declaration of payments. Controlled waste needs European Waste Catalogue (EWC) codes, waste transfer notes and evidence that the waste was sorted away from higher-value uses. The counterfactual fate of the feedstock needs a three- or five-year look-back, and market leakage must be either quantified or shown to be zero (RDF is a named example of feedstock surplus). Where there are more than 10 source lots, Isometric must approve a sampling plan before evidence collection begins ([Biomass Feedstock Accounting v1.4](https://registry.isometric.com/module/biomass-feedstock-accounting)). BioCCS also requires a waste hierarchy showing that only unrecyclable waste is burned (`R-Q8V2-0`).

### Every truckload needs a ticket that reconciles capture with injection

Liquefied CO2 moved by road creates a custody gap between the capture meter and the wellhead. GSC 7.4.4(b) requires documentation from the logistics operator of the tonnes delivered to the storage site. The calibration tolerance across the chain is 5% (7.2.8–7.2.9), and any leak downstream of the last meter must be estimated as E_released ([GSC 2024 v5](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Geologically_Stored_Carbon_2024_5.pdf)). Isometric sets the tolerance at 2%. It allows batch weighing or tank gauging "with records at loading and receipt", requires leak detection with audible alarms on interim storage and transport, and prefers transport emissions calculated from fuel invoices over distance-based estimates ([BioCCS v1.5](https://registry.isometric.com/protocol/biogenic-capture-and-storage); [GHG Accounting v1.1](https://registry.isometric.com/module/ghg-accounting)). Both registries therefore need three reconciled metering points: the capture outlet, the truck loading and unloading tickets, and the injection wellhead.

### Mineralisation storage is judged on bubble point, permits and jurisdiction

Both methodologies accept dissolved-CO2 injection into mafic and ultramafic rock, and both use the same physical test: pressure in the reservoir must stay **more than 5 bar above the bubble point**, checked at least monthly. On Puro, CO2 injected while that margin is breached counts as a reversal. GSC also requires continuous CO2 and water mass flow, quarterly checks of composition and CO2 fraction, monthly leak inspection, periodic corrosion monitoring, and a site characterisation. It sets no minimum mineralisation rate ([GSC 2024 v5](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Geologically_Stored_Carbon_2024_5.pdf)).

Isometric's module is far more prescriptive. Its baseline table requires divalent cation content, reactive surface area, a regional seismicity study and a PHREEQC bubble-point calculation. Its operational schedule runs from continuous measurements down to daily injectate pH, weekly formation-fluid checks, monthly reactive-tracer sampling, quarterly corrosion checks, annual mechanical integrity tests and a pressure fall-off test every two years. **Post-injection monitoring lasts at least 50 years** unless mineralisation is proven sooner, and closure requires proof that the CO2 stays within the area of review over more than 100,000 years (`R-G5BD-0`) ([In-situ Module v1.2](https://registry.isometric.com/module/in-situ-mineralization/1.2)).

Jurisdiction is the decisive difference. GSC 3.2.11 accepts storage outright only in the USA, the EEA, the UK, Alberta, Saskatchewan and British Columbia. Anywhere else the host country must show that its framework meets all eight rows of Table 1: designed for permanent storage, a storage permit, site characterisation, a defined CO2 stream, monitoring, periodic reporting, emergency response, and closure with financial responsibility. "Puro.earth reserves the right to determine the eligibility" ([GSC 2024 v5](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Geologically_Stored_Carbon_2024_5.pdf)). The audit index instructs suppliers to obtain Puro's **written approval of this evidence "prior to the audit"** ([local audit package](../docs/1.%20Final%20Submission/)). Isometric's approved permitting regimes are US Class VI, the EU CCS Directive, Norway, the UK and Alberta Directive 064. Outside them, a licensed geoscientist signs a seven-part report covering site characterisation, area-of-review modelling, the risk register, the monitoring plan, well integrity, equivalence to the listed permits, and a maturity opinion under the Storage Resources Management System (SRMS) ([In-situ Module v1.2](https://registry.isometric.com/module/in-situ-mineralization/1.2)). Puro's test is a policy decision by the registry. Isometric's is an engineering attestation the supplier can commission. Separately, Puro requires proof that the storage site is approved for permanent geological storage (7.5.1). Local no-objection certificates will be tested against that requirement and may fall short.

## Conclusion

Choosing between the registries means choosing which uncertainty to carry. On Puro it is a legal-policy question decided early and at the registry's discretion: will Puro accept UAE storage law against Table 1? Puro has already settled the harder question of whether a cement kiln qualifies, and the package is already shaped for Puro. On Isometric it is a technical question that the supplier can work on but not finish: whether cement qualifies under BioCCS at all, plus heavier radiocarbon, monitoring and post-closure obligations. Because the repository has already committed to Puro GSC, the critical path is now three written rulings to obtain before any audit booking: approval of the Table 1 legal framework, a ruling on whether the 3-year or the 5-year validation window applies to a Commitment Date that has most likely already passed, and the categorisation of tyre-derived biogenic carbon.

The less obvious implication concerns sequencing between the two registries. Puro refuses projects that "failed a certification audit under another certification scheme within the previous three (3) years" (CP 9.8.21), and once a project is validated its credits "must remain exclusive" ([CP v1.2](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Supplier%20Documents/Puro%20Earth%20Certification%20Procedures%20v1.2%20FINAL.pdf)). Isometric is therefore a fallback only if Puro says no before an audit, not after a failed one. In the meantime, an Isometric eligibility report on the cement source is a low-commitment hedge. It is a desk review of design documents, not a VVB audit. To be safe it should be disclosed in Puro's Project Concept Note, which asks about earlier certification attempts. In return it would show whether Isometric's more generous five-year look-back on past injections is actually open to this facility.
